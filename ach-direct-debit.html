ACH Direct Debit

Accept payments by debiting your US customers' bank accounts.

Overview

Enable in the Dashboard

ACH Direct Debit lets you collect payments directly from a customer's US bank account. It's one of the most popular payment methods in the United States for recurring and high-value transactions due to its low cost and broad reach across virtually all US bank accounts.

Settlement and timing

ACH Direct Debit is a delayed-notification payment method. After a customer authorizes a payment, it can take up to 4 business days to confirm whether the payment was successful.

How settlement work

When a customer completes an ACH payment, the PaymentIntent status moves to processing. During this window, the bank can reject the debit for reasons such as insufficient funds, an invalid account, or a disabled account. If the payment succeeds, the status changes to succeeded.

Because of the delayed confirmation, avoid fulfilling orders until the payment status reaches succeeded. If a payment fails after being initially accepted, Stripe removes the funds from your Stripe balance.

Bank account verification

Before debiting a customer's account, the bank account must be verified. Stripe supports two verification methods:

  • Instant verification — Uses Financial Connections to verify the account immediately. This is recommended as it reduces fraud and payment failures.
  • Microdeposits — Two small deposits are sent to the bank account and the customer verifies the amounts. This process takes 1-2 business days and customers have a 10-day window to complete verification.

Refunds

ACH Direct Debit supports full and partial refunds, and you can issue multiple refunds against a single payment as long as the total refunded amount doesn't exceed the original charge.

Refund window and processing time

You have up to 180 days from the original payment to submit a refund through Stripe. Your return policy takes precedence — the 180-day window is the maximum allowed by the payment method, not a guarantee to your customers.

Refunds require at least 3 business days to process. Customers typically see the refund in their bank account within 5–10 business days. The refund appears as a separate credit on the customer's bank statement with a reference to the original payment's statement descriptor — not as a reversal of the original charge.

Issuing a refund

Submit refunds using the Dashboard or the Refunds API. When you submit a refund, Stripe sends the request through the ACH network to the customer's bank. The bank credits the refund to the customer's original bank account.

Refunds for ACH Direct Debit payments that haven't completed yet (status: processing) can't be submitted until the payment succeeds. If a payment fails, no refund is necessary since the funds were never collected.

Canceling a refund

Merchants can cancel a refund while it's still processing through the Dashboard or the Refunds API (/v1/refunds/{refund}/cancel).

Tracking refund status

In the Dashboard, filter the Payments page by refunded, refund_pending, or partially_refunded to view refund details. Programmatically, retrieve refund status through the Refunds API or listen for refund.updated and refund.failed webhook events.

Refund notifications

Stripe does not notify merchants when an ACH refund succeeds — listen for the refund.updated webhook event to track status changes. Stripe does send a refund.failed webhook event if a refund can't be processed. In failure cases, the refund amount is returned to your Stripe balance and you must return funds to the customer outside of Stripe.

Avoiding double credits

If you issue a refund while the customer's bank simultaneously initiates a dispute, the customer could receive two credits for the same transaction. Contact the customer immediately if you accidentally debit them, and coordinate before issuing a refund on a payment that may also be disputed.

Disputes

Customers can dispute ACH Direct Debit payments through their bank. When a payment is disputed, the customer's bank initiates an ACH return and Stripe immediately deducts the disputed amount and fee from your Stripe balance.

Dispute window

Customers can dispute ACH Direct Debit payments within 60 calendar days for personal bank accounts and 2 business days for business bank accounts.

In rare instances, a debit payment can be successfully disputed outside these timelines. This is called a late return. The late return process is primarily managed by and ultimately decided at the discretion of the banks involved in the transaction.

Dispute notifications

Stripe notifies you of disputes by email, in the Dashboard under the Disputes tab, and through the charge.dispute.created webhook event. You can view the dispute reason in the Dashboard or through the reason attribute on the Dispute API object.

Responding to disputes

ACH Direct Debit disputes are final and cannot be appealed. You can't contest standard ACH reversals through Stripe — contact the customer directly to resolve the issue.

The one exception is ACH authorization inquiries (late returns). For these, Stripe creates a dispute with a warning_needs_response status and you can submit evidence. Upload a copy of the mandate authorization, sales receipt, and customer information as an image or PDF through the Dashboard or the Files API. If you don't submit evidence before the deadline, the dispute is accepted and closed.

After receiving evidence submissions, Stripe sends a charge.dispute.updated event and a message appears in your timeline confirming receipt of the evidence. The bank then reviews the evidence and makes a determination. If the evidence is sufficient, the dispute inquiry closes in your favor and is marked as won. If the bank determines that the authorization is insufficient, the dispute closes and is marked as lost. Stripe doesn't determine whether or not evidence of authorization is sufficient, and under Nacha rules, the bank may return the payment in its sole discretion.

Fees

Stripe charges a dispute fee, which varies by your business location. Dispute fees are not refunded, even if you successfully contest an authorization inquiry. View pricing here.

Impact on future payments

A disputed ACH Direct Debit payment invalidates the mandate associated with the payment method, so you can't reuse that bank account. If additional disputes occur, Stripe blocks the bank account from further use.

Transaction failures

ACH Direct Debit transactions can fail any time after the payment is initiated through payment confirmation. These failures can occur for a number of reasons, such as:

  • Insufficient funds
  • An invalid account number
  • A customer disabling debits from their bank account

If a payment fails after funds have been made available in your Stripe balance, Stripe immediately removes funds from your Stripe account.

In rare situations, Stripe might receive an ACH failure from the bank after a PaymentIntent has transitioned to succeeded. If this happens, Stripe creates a dispute with a reason of:

  • insufficient_funds
  • incorrect_account_details
  • bank_cannot_process

Stripe charges a failure fee in this situation.

Mandates

To debit a customer's bank account with ACH, you must first obtain their authorization through a mandate. A mandate is a record of the permission that a customer gives you to debit their bank account.

Stripe handles mandate collection as part of the payment flow when you use Checkout, Payment Links, or the Payment Element. The customer reviews the mandate terms and accepts them during the payment process. After the customer authorizes the mandate, Stripe sends a confirmation email to the customer per Nacha compliance requirements.

Mandate types

ACH supports both online and offline mandates:

  • Online mandates — Collected digitally through your payment flow. Stripe manages these automatically when using Checkout, Payment Links, or the Payment Element.
  • Offline mandates — Collected through paper forms or phone calls. If you collect mandates offline, you're responsible for storing the authorization and providing it if a dispute arises.

For detailed information on mandate requirements, verification methods, and compliance considerations, see the Accept a payment guide.

Transaction identifiers

After a customer completes an ACH Direct Debit payment, the transaction appears on their bank statement with your Stripe account's statement descriptor. You can override this default behavior for every transaction independently by using a dynamic statement descriptor. To do so, specify the statement_descriptor parameter when creating the PaymentIntent.

Use a clear, recognizable statement descriptor to help customers identify the payment and reduce the likelihood of disputes. The descriptor can be up to 16 characters for ACH payments.

Each bank formats these fields differently. Depending on your customer's bank, some fields might appear in all lowercase or all uppercase.

Enable ACH Direct Debit

If you use our front-end products, you can enable ACH Direct Debit directly from your payment method settings — no additional integration required. Stripe then automatically determines the most relevant payment methods to display to your customers.

If your integration requires manually listing payment methods, learn how to manually configure ACH Direct Debit as a payment.
